# Angles: from a topic to a concept worth scoring

A topic is a subject ("airplane food"). A concept is a topic plus an angle that gives a stranger an
aha, a reason to send it, and a reason to want more ("At cruising altitude your sense of salt and
sweet drops by about a third, so the same meal that tastes fine on the ground tastes of nothing in
the air"). This file has the moves for both
directions: building concepts from a topic, and re-angling a concept the gate sent back.

## 1. Aha angles

For every topic that survives the topic board, try all six and keep the two or three with the
strongest Before / After line.

| Angle | The move | Before → After, for "airplane food" |
|---|---|---|
| **Absurd but true** | Find the strangest true thing about the topic and lead with it | "Airline food is just food" → "Some airlines test meals in a pressurised tube that copies a cabin, because food that tastes right on the ground tastes wrong in the air" |
| **The hidden why** | Ask why the familiar thing is the way it is, and find the real reason | "Airline food is bad because it's cheap" → "Dry cabin air and low pressure numb your taste for salt and sweet, so even good food tastes bland up there" |
| **Reframe** | Show the familiar thing from an angle that changes what it means | "Tomato juice on planes is a weird habit" → "Tomato juice tastes better in the air, so people who never drink it on the ground order it" |
| **Reversal** | Find where the obvious answer is wrong | "Fancy airline food tastes better" → "Strong, plain flavours survive at altitude; delicate dishes are the ones that taste of nothing" |
| **Scale shock** | One number that resets the viewer's sense of size | "A plane carries some meals" → "A big airline kitchen can make tens of thousands of meals a day" (check the figure before using it) |
| **Hidden link** | Connect the topic to something that seems unrelated | "It's the air" → "It's also the engine noise: loud noise dulls sweetness and sharpens savoury flavours, part of why tomato juice tastes better on board" |

Rules:

- **Check the claim before building on it.** An aha that turns out false is worse than no aha (see
  the truth cap in `entertainment-rubric.md`). Note the source on the concept card.
- **Familiar beats exotic.** The best surprises are about things the viewer touches every week.
- **One surprise per concept.** Two competing reveals blur the share line.

## 2. Share angles

Write the share message first, then shape the concept so it earns it.

| Motive | Shape the concept so that… | Example move |
|---|---|---|
| **Look smart** | The fact is short enough to repeat at dinner, and true | Reduce the reveal to one sentence with one number |
| **This is so you** | A specific type of person sees themselves in it | Build it around "the friend who always [habit]" |
| **You need this** | The viewer can act on it today | End the reveal on what to do differently ("order the tomato juice") |
| **Can you believe this** | The feeling is too big to keep | Raise the stakes: who it affects, how long it's been going on |
| **Settle this** | There is a real side to take | Frame it as a question people genuinely disagree on, and give your answer |
| **LOL** | The absurdity is visible, not explained | Show the dog in the sheriff's hat; don't describe it |

A concept with two motives (look smart + LOL, you need this + can you believe this) is usually the
strongest.

## 3. More angles

- **Find the bigger question.** After the payoff, what would a curious viewer ask next? Make sure the
  concept lets them feel it: "and it's not the only town that did this."
- **Name episode 2 and episode 10.** If you can't, the concept is a one-off. That's allowed, but it
  scores low on Series.
- **Make it the channel's.** What does this channel bring that others can't: access, expertise, a
  recurring character, a take? Put it in the concept, not just in the delivery.
- **For news: own the take.** The event is everyone's; the hidden why, the consequence nobody
  mentions, or a clear opinion is yours.

## 4. Re-angle moves, by weak dimension

When the gate returns REWORK, give three re-angles. Each names the dimension it lifts and the score
you expect after it. Pick moves from the rows that match the weak dimensions.

| Weak dimension | Moves |
|---|---|
| **Who cares** | Zoom out to the stake everyone has (money, body, safety, family). Tie the niche thing to an everyday object. Add a person with something to lose. |
| **Fresh** | Change the angle, not the topic: from "what" to "why", from "how" to "who loses". Find the newest fact in the story. Move from the crowded explanation to the consequence nobody covers. |
| **Surprise** | Find the "before" belief and aim at it. Switch to a reversal or a hidden-why angle. Swap a general claim for its strangest specific case. |
| **Click** | Cut to one mechanism and one picture. Replace jargon with the everyday object it describes. Verify the claim, or frame it honestly as a theory. |
| **Share motive** | Write the message first, then rebuild the concept to earn it. Add a recipient ("the friend who…"). Add a takeaway the viewer can use. Turn a statement into a real debate. |
| **Arousal** | Raise the stakes (who's affected, what's at risk). Make the absurd visible. Add a turn: disbelief into laughter, dread into relief. |
| **Depth** | Point to the bigger system behind this case. Add "and it's not the only one". Leave the open question that the payoff reveals. |
| **Series and ownership** | Turn the one-off into a format ("animals with real jobs", "foods that change in the air"). Add what only this channel can bring. |

## 5. Worked re-angles

### The like trap

**Concept:** "A tour of a cute small town where everyone knows each other." Pleasant, broad, nothing
new: Who cares 6, Fresh 4, Surprise 2, Click 5, Share motive 3, Arousal 3, Depth 2, Series 3 (total
about 35).

Re-angles:

1. **Absurd but true + LOL:** "This town has had a dog for mayor for over 20 years, and it holds
   real elections." Surprise 2 → 8, Share motive 3 → 8 (look smart + LOL), Arousal 3 → 7. (Find
   the real town and check it before building on it.)
2. **Hidden why + you need this:** "Why this town has no traffic lights, and fewer crashes than
   yours." Surprise 2 → 7, Click 5 → 7, Share motive 3 → 6.
3. **Series:** "Towns with one weird rule, part 1." Series 3 → 7, Depth 2 → 6.

### Dead-end news

**Concept:** "Summary of today's big court ruling." Who cares 7, Fresh 8, Surprise 3, Depth 2,
Series 2.

Re-angles: the hidden why ("the one sentence in the ruling that changes your rent"); the consequence
nobody covers; a clear, owned take that viewers will argue with (Settle this). Each lifts Surprise and
Share motive, and the consequence or the take lifts Depth and Series.

### Trivia nobody asked for

**Concept:** "A rare moss only grows on one island." Surprise 6, Who cares 2.

Re-angles: tie it to an everyday stake ("the moss in your garden is the reason your house isn't
flooding"), or to a hidden link the viewer cares about. If neither works honestly, KILL it.

## 6. Writing the concept card

Use `assets/concept-card.md`. A card is ready for the gate when it has:

- the premise in one sentence a 10-year-old could repeat;
- the Before → After line;
- the share message, its recipient and its motive;
- the bigger question it leaves, and episode 2;
- the evidence: sources for the claim, the scout numbers for freshness, with dates;
- the risks: what still needs checking, what might not be producible.

The card describes the idea, not the video. Leave hook lines, first frames and shot lists to
Hooks.
