## 1. What the score judges, and what it leaves alone

The entertainment score asks one question: **if this is told well, will a stranger get an aha, want
to send it to someone, and want more?** That is the difference between a video that gets a like and
stops, and one that travels.

It judges **the idea**: the topic and the angle. It does not judge the telling. Hook wording, the
first frame, pacing, cuts and the ending's form are the Hooks skill's job, and a concept is never marked
down for a weak hook line that a rewrite would fix. The test for every note you write: *could an
edit fix this?* If yes, it belongs to Hooks, not here.

The score is reported on its own. It is never added to a Hooks review score, because the two
measure different things and the Hooks skill's bars were calibrated without it.

## 2. The eight dimensions

Score each from 0 to 10, then weight. Total = sum of (score / 10 x weight), out of 100.

| # | Dimension | Group | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Who cares | Foundation | 15 |
| 2 | Fresh | Foundation | 10 |
| 3 | Surprise | Aha | 15 |
| 4 | Click | Aha | 10 |
| 5 | Share motive | Share | 20 |
| 6 | Arousal | Share | 10 |
| 7 | Depth | More | 10 |
| 8 | Series and ownership | More | 10 |

Share motive carries the most weight on purpose. Instagram has said publicly that sends per reach
are among its top ranking signals and count most for reaching people who don't follow you; see
`why-people-share.md` for what is known and how sure to be.

### 1. Who cares (15)

**The test:** name three different strangers who would stop for this, each with their reason.
Insiders and existing fans don't count.

- Does it touch something the viewer has a stake in: their body, money, food, home, phone, kids,
  pets, job, safety, a place they know, fairness or status?
- Does caring need prior knowledge (a game, a fandom, a hobby's jargon)?
- A fun fact nobody asked about fails here, however true or odd it is.

Anchors: **0-2** only insiders care, or caring needs knowledge most people lack. **3-4** a hobby or
fandom niche; outsiders scroll on. **5-6** a broad group finds it mildly interesting, but it isn't
personal. **7-8** most people have a direct stake or a personal link. **9-10** universal and personal
at once: everyone has this thing, and it touches them.

### 2. Fresh (10)

**The test:** how many recent videos make this exact point, and did they work? Has this channel made
it already? For news and commentary: is the window still open?

Use evidence. `scripts/scout.py search` gives how many recent Shorts make the point and how they
did. The channel ledger gives what this channel has made. Without either, score Fresh at most 6 and
write "unchecked" next to it.

Freshness is relative to the audience: a fact everyone in one community knows can be new to another.
Crowded and working means the angle needs to change; crowded and flat means the topic was tried and
strangers didn't care.

Anchors: **0-2** the feed is full of this exact point, the channel made it recently, or the news
window has closed. **3-4** a common topic told the usual way. **5-6** a common topic with a new
angle, or a rare topic told the usual way. **7-8** rare in the feed, or a fresh angle on a rising
topic. **9-10** the viewer has almost certainly not seen it, and now is the moment: newly found,
newly true, or rising in search this week.

### 3. Surprise (15)

**The test:** write *"Before, viewers think ___. After, they know ___."* If the two halves say the
same thing, there is no aha. If the viewer had no "before" at all, nothing breaks.

Kinds of surprise (name the one that applies; `angles.md` has the moves for each):

- **Absurd but true.** "Wait, that's real?" A town has elected a dog as its mayor.
- **The hidden why.** An everyday thing has a reason nobody told you.
- **Reframe.** You've been seeing something familiar the wrong way.
- **Reversal.** The obvious answer is wrong.
- **Scale shock.** One number that resets the viewer's sense of size, cost or time.
- **Hidden link.** Two things that seem unrelated turn out to be connected.

Surprise is strongest about familiar things: a viewer needs an expectation for it to break, and
people are most curious when they half know the answer.

Anchors: **0-2** the viewer knows it already or would guess it. **3-4** a "huh" forgotten in a
second. **5-6** a real surprise, but about something the viewer had no expectation of, so nothing
breaks. **7-8** breaks an expectation most people hold about something familiar. **9-10** overturns
something the viewer sees every day; they will think of it the next time they see it.

### 4. Click (10)

**The test:** retell the why in one breath to a 10-year-old. Then check it is true.

- Does the explanation resolve the surprise, or leave it at "nobody knows"?
- Can it be said without jargon or a chain of steps? Deep topics are welcome; the bar is how simply
  the core idea can be said, not how simple the topic is.
- Is there a concrete picture the viewer can see (a mechanism, an object, a before and after)?

**Truth cap.** If the core claim cannot be checked, or is speculation presented as fact, Click is
capped at 4 and the report says so in its first line. An open mystery honestly framed as a theory
("one explanation is…") can reach 6. A source the user supplies counts as checked; say it was not
re-checked. A fact you know well from reliable sources counts too; name where it comes from. Never
help a concept pass on a claim nobody has verified.

Anchors: **0-2** no explanation, or the claim is false, or unverifiable and stated as fact. **3-4**
the explanation needs jargon or several steps, or ends at "experts disagree". **5-6** clear but flat:
a fact stated, with no picture of how it works. **7-8** one mechanism, said in one breath, with a
concrete picture. **9-10** the explanation is itself the delight: once seen it can't be unseen, and
the viewer could teach it at dinner.

### 5. Share motive (20)

**The test:** write the message the sharer types when sending it, and who it goes to. If you can only
write "nice" or "lol", it is a like, not a share.

Name the motive, or two (details and examples in `why-people-share.md`):

| Motive | What the sender is saying |
|---|---|
| **Look smart** | "Did you know…?" Sending it makes me look informed. |
| **This is so you** | A tag or a DM to one specific person: it's about them, or us. |
| **You need this** | Useful: it saves money, time or trouble, or it's a warning. |
| **Can you believe this** | Awe or outrage too big to keep to myself. |
| **Settle this** | A debate, a side to take: "who's right?" |
| **LOL** | Funny enough that sharing the laugh is the point. |

- Does sending it make the sender look good (smart, funny, caring, first to know)?
- Would the recipient reply, or pass it on? A share that starts a conversation is worth more.
- Is it safe to send? Content people like but would be embarrassed to forward gets likes, not sends.

Anchors: **0-2** no message can be written, or only "nice". **3-4** a generic "lol" or "cute" to
anyone, interchangeable with a thousand videos. **5-6** a clear motive, but the message is bland or
the sender gains nothing by sending it. **7-8** a specific recipient and message, and sending it
makes the sender look good. **9-10** two motives at once, and the recipient will reply or pass it on.

### 6. Arousal (10)

**The test:** name the feeling and how strong it is. Strength matters more than whether it is
pleasant.

- **High:** awe, laughing out loud, disbelief ("no way"), indignation at an unfairness, dread then
  relief, being moved (chills, a lump in the throat).
- **Low:** contentment, calm, a mild "aww", mild interest, sadness.

**Rage bait.** Outrage aimed at a private person or a group, or outrage manufactured from a claim
that isn't true, is flagged, not rewarded. It usually also fails Click.

Anchors: **0-2** flat and informational. **3-4** mild and pleasant: "aww", "nice", "huh". This is
where likes live. **5-6** one clear feeling of moderate strength. **7-8** a strong feeling the viewer
could name: they laugh, say "no way", or get angry at something unfair. **9-10** a strong feeling
that turns: disbelief into laughter, dread into relief, outrage into justice.

### 7. Depth (10)

**The test:** after the payoff, what bigger question does the viewer now have? Write it down.

- A good answer reveals a bigger gap: learning a little makes people aware of what they don't know.
- Is the topic a door to a world (more cases, the system behind this one, the history)?
- News you finish and never seek more of fails here: it closes everything it opens.

Anchors: **0-2** closed: once known, there is nothing more to want. **3-4** a related question
exists, but the concept doesn't make the viewer feel it. **5-6** the viewer would happily watch one
more on this. **7-8** the payoff opens a bigger question the viewer now wants answered. **9-10** a
whole world opens, and the viewer goes looking: searches, the channel page, the comments.

### 8. Series and ownership (10)

**The test:** is there an episode 2, and an episode 10? And the commodity test: if ten channels
posted this same video today, would it matter which one you saw?

- A repeatable format turns one aha into a reason to follow: "every week, an animal with a real job".
- Ownership is what only this channel brings: access, expertise, a recurring character, a voice, a
  take. Commentary on a news event is owned when the take is the channel's own; reading the news is
  not.
- Without channel context, score repeatability only, cap at 6, and say so.

Anchors: **0-2** a one-off that anyone could make identically. **3-4** repeatable but generic:
nothing ties it to this channel. **5-6** a format with an obvious episode 2, and some channel
identity. **7-8** a clear series promise and a distinctive angle, access or voice. **9-10** the
viewer could name what this channel does and wants the next one.

## 3. Outcome scores

Report three outcome scores out of 10 next to the total. They answer the user's three questions
directly, and they show *where* a total comes from: a 72 with Share 4 is a different problem from a
72 with More 4.

- **Aha** = (Surprise x 15 + Click x 10) / 25
- **Share** = (Share motive x 20 + Arousal x 10) / 30
- **More** = (Depth + Series and ownership) / 2

Report the foundation as its two scores (Who cares, Fresh), not combined.

## 4. Profiles

After scoring, check the concept against these shapes and name the one that fits. A profile tells
the user what kind of problem they have, which a number cannot.

| Profile | Shape | Typical case | What to do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Like trap** | Who cares 5+, Arousal 3-4, Surprise ≤ 4, Share motive ≤ 4 | Pleasant, wholesome, nothing new: viewers like it and scroll on | REWORK toward a share motive (`angles.md`), or KILL |
| **Dead-end news** | Who cares and Fresh 6+, Depth and Series ≤ 3 | News watched to the end and never followed up | Add an owned take or the hidden why, and the bigger question it opens |
| **Trivia nobody asked for** | Surprise 5+, Who cares ≤ 3 | A true, odd fact with no reason to care | Tie it to something people do care about, or KILL |
| **Smart but cold** | Surprise and Click 7+, Arousal ≤ 4 | A clever explanation that nobody feels | Add a person with something at stake, or a consequence |
| **Rage bait** | Arousal 7+, Click ≤ 4 | Outrage on a shaky claim | Flag it; don't greenlight on the strength of the anger |
| **Insider hit** | Share motive 7+, Who cares ≤ 4 | Huge inside a niche, invisible outside it | Pass only when the user says the channel serves this niche on purpose, and say the reach ceiling |
| **Hit shape** | Surprise 7+, Share motive 7+, Who cares 7+, and Depth or Series 6+ | An absurd-but-true fact people send on; event commentary with a real take; a deep topic made simple | GREENLIGHT |

## 5. Verdict rules and the bar

Pick exactly one. Work down the table and take the first that applies:

| Verdict | When |
|---|---|
| **KILL** | Who cares ≤ 3, or Fresh ≤ 2, or the core claim is false. The topic goes; back to research. One exception: a channel that serves a niche on purpose can take an insider hit (section 4), judged on the other floors. |
| **REWORK** | The total is below the bar, or Share motive < 6, or Aha < 6 while Arousal < 7. The topic stays and the angle changes. Give three re-angles from `angles.md`, each naming the dimension it lifts and the new score you expect. At most two REWORK rounds per topic, then KILL. |
| **GREENLIGHT** | The total is at or above the bar, Share motive ≥ 6, and Aha ≥ 6 or Arousal ≥ 7. |

The floors matter more than the total. A concept can reach 70 on breadth and freshness alone and
still be a like trap; the Share motive floor stops it.

### The bar

| Total | Reading |
|---|---|
| **75+** | Strong. Greenlight if the floors hold. |
| **70-74** | At the bar. Greenlight if the floors hold. |
| **60-69** | Below the bar. REWORK: one re-angle usually moves a concept 5-10 points. |
| **Below 60** | Well below. REWORK only if one dimension is dragging everything down; otherwise KILL. |

- **The 70 bar is a starting guess, not a measured line.** The score has not been checked against
  real results yet. Until it has, treat totals within 3 points of 70 (67-73) as borderline: say so
  in the verdict line, then try the top re-angle or average two more blind scores.
- **Score blind when you can.** Whoever wrote a concept is the worst person to score it. When a
  subagent is available, score in a fresh one that sees only the concept card.
- **Never predict views.** Say which signal the concept should move (sends, follows, views ÷ subs)
  and stop there.

## 6. Script check

When a script exists, run this before it goes to the Hooks skill's polish. The point is to catch value
lost in writing: a strong concept can still turn into a script that buries the aha.

1. **Re-score the eight dimensions on the script.** Any dimension that drops by 2 or more from the
   concept score is a finding: name the line or the missing line that caused it.
2. **Aha line.** Quote the line where the surprise lands, with its rough timestamp. Is it said
   plainly, in the payoff? Is it given away so early that nothing is left to land? (Teasing it in the
   hook is fine; answering it in the hook is not.)
3. **Share line.** Quote the one sentence a viewer would repeat when sending it. Is it in the script,
   said plainly, and on screen? If no such sentence exists, write one.
4. **More pull.** Does the payoff leave the bigger question from Depth? It should come from the
   content itself, never a subscribe ask; the ending's form stays the Hooks skill's call.

Script verdicts: **PASS** (nothing dropped by 2+, and all three lines are present) or **FIX** (list
each fix as the exact line to add, cut or move). A script never gets KILL here: the concept already
passed.

## 7. Video mode

Score a finished video's topic from its transcript, key frames and title. Use it when a Hooks
review asks whether the topic itself was worth it, and for calibration.

- Judge the content as delivered, ignoring execution. A great topic badly told scores high here and
  low in Hooks; that split is exactly what the user needs to see.
- Fresh is judged as of the upload date, so note it. Series and ownership uses the channel's other
  uploads if you have them.
- Report it as its own section, never merged into the Hooks skill's total.

## 8. Evidence format

Every dimension score comes with its evidence in one or two lines:

- Who cares: the three strangers and their reasons.
- Fresh: the search and its result, with the date. `(scout search "dog sheriff", 2026-11-02: 2
  breakouts of 15, newest 400 days old)`, or "unchecked".
- Surprise: the Before / After line, and the kind of surprise.
- Click: the one-breath why, and the source checked (or "unverified", with the cap applied).
- Share motive: the message, the recipient and the motive. `"you HAVE to see this town" → the
  friend who's always on about small-town news (look smart + LOL)`.
- Arousal: the feeling and its strength.
- Depth: the bigger question, written as the viewer would ask it.
- Series: episode 2, and what makes it this channel's.
