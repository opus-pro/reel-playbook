# Hook-story playbook (Create mode)

The order matters: **story wrapper, then hooks, then the story built backwards from the hook's
promise, then the script, then the first frame and shots, then a paper review.** Writing the hook
first forces the story to be about something a stranger will stop for. Building backwards from the
promise makes sure the ending pays it off.

## 1. Intake

Ask only for what is missing:

- Topic, product or idea; who it is for; the platform; the language.
- What can actually be produced: people, places, props, footage, budget, whether there is AI
  generation. A hook is only as good as the shot that pays it off.
- Whether the creator's face is a draw (an established audience) or a stranger's face (then the
  object or logo usually travels further in frame 1).
- The target length. Default to about 33-34 s, Jenny's observed sweet spot, and treat it as a
  hypothesis.

## 2. Find the story: wrappers

A topic becomes a story when someone wants something for a reason. Run the topic through every lens
below and write at least 6 wrappers, each in one sentence.

| Lens | Shape | Example from a plain topic |
|---|---|---|
| **Stakes / reason** | "X happened, so I have to Y" | "Cooking" becomes "My kitchen broke, so I have to cook my way to the repair money" |
| **Constraint** | "Only $1 / 1 hour / one ingredient / no tools" | "Fast food" becomes "Recreating a famous burger for $1" |
| **Doubter** | "Someone says it can't be done, so I prove it" | "Cheap gifts" becomes "Grandma says gifts are too expensive, so I'm getting her a $5 one" |
| **Test with a hidden answer** | "Blind test, ranking or compare, and we wait for the verdict" | "Coffee" becomes "My barista friend blind-tastes $1 vs $8 coffee" |
| **Zoom out** | "A niche thing as a window onto a big world question" | "Phone review" becomes "The chip war: can this phone break the blockade?" |
| **Transformation** | "Before, then a process, then after" | "Desk setup" becomes "Turning a $20 thrift desk into this" |
| **Mystery** | "Why does X? Here's the answer, at the end" | "Ketchup" becomes "Why every diner bottle is glass" |

Keep a wrapper only if:
- a 10-year-old can retell it in one breath;
- the payoff is something you can **show**, such as a reaction, a verdict, a reveal or a number on screen;
- it can actually be produced with the user's resources;
- someone outside the niche would care. If not, try the zoom-out or doubter lens.

## 3. Hooks first

### The hook package

A hook is four things, designed together:

| Part | Spec |
|---|---|
| **Line** | Spoken, about 2 s or less: roughly 8-10 words. Contains the stakes, not just the topic. |
| **Frame 1** | The poster: one subject, one context cue, high contrast, readable small. It is a real frame of the video, not a title card. |
| **On-screen text** | Three words or one number. It carries the hook for muted viewers and stays out of the UI zones. |
| **Preview** | Which payoff moment flashes in at about 1-2.5 s (0.5-1 s long) while the line plays. |

### Line patterns

Adapt these; don't fill them in mechanically. For the full hook bank (14 families, each with example
lines), the hook tests and before-and-after rewrites, see `hooks-and-rehooks.md`. Spread
the 10+ packages across at least four families.

- "I [made / bought / tried] [X] with only [$1 / one hour / one ingredient]."
- "My [person] says [doubt]. So I [action]."
- "[Small object]. [Big world stake]. Can it [do Y]?"
- "This one costs [$], this one costs [$$$]. [Person] has to pick, blind."
- "I have [constraint] to [goal], or [consequence]."
- "Everyone does [X]. Here's why that's wrong." (Only if the payoff proves it.)

Banned openers: greetings, names, "in this video", "today I'm going to", "let's get started",
"wait for it", questions with obvious answers ("Have you ever wanted to save money?"), and a logo,
countdown or warm-up shot in frame 1. If cutting the first second loses nothing, it was warm-up.

### Scoring hooks

Score each package from 0 to 2 on seven checks, for a maximum of 14:

| Check | 2 = | 0 = |
|---|---|---|
| **Clear** | A 10-year-old gets it at once | Needs context or jargon |
| **Stakes** | There is a reason, goal or consequence | A topic only |
| **Gap** | Raises a question the viewer wants answered | Answers itself |
| **Muted** | Frame 1 and the text carry it without sound | Needs the audio |
| **Payable** | The payoff exists or can be shot, and it is visual | Cannot be delivered |
| **Broad** | Non-fans would care | Insiders only |
| **Fast** | Lands in 2 s or less | Takes 4 s or more |

Discard any package that scores 0 on Payable, whatever the total. A hook the video cannot pay off is
clickbait, and clickbait collapses retention. Discard anything under 9. Keep the winner and a
runner-up as an alternative.

## 4. Story backwards from the promise

### Order of construction

1. **Payoff (the last 2-3 s).** The exact answer to the hook's question, shown visually: a reaction,
   verdict, reveal or result. It is the shot the preview borrows.
2. **Mechanism (the middle).** Pick one from the menu below. It must make the process worth
   watching, so that skipping to the end loses something.
3. **Setup (about 2.5-6 s).** Make the stakes and constraint concrete. Show the broken kitchen;
   don't explain it.
4. **The hook (0-2.5 s).** It is already written. Check that it still matches the payoff exactly.

### Mechanism menu

- **Shrinking or escalating constraint:** a timer, a budget running down, a circle shrinking.
- **Comparison with a hidden answer:** a blind test or a ranking. The verdict waits for the end.
- **Steps or countdown:** "3 steps", "5 tries". Step 1 must be good enough to buy steps 2 and 3.
- **A visible counter or progress:** money earned so far, a score, a progress bar on screen.
- **Bet or prediction:** "Guess before the end" or "I bet $X that...".
- **A rule change mid-way:** a new obstacle at about 60% that resets the stakes.

### Beat sheet, about 33.5 s (scale it for other lengths)

| Time | Beat | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | **Frame 1** | The poster spec, live from the very first frame. No fade. |
| 0.0-2.0 | **Hook line** + on-screen text | Voice starts on frame 1. |
| 1.0-2.5 | **Preview** of the payoff (0.5-1 s), then cut back | The hook line keeps playing over it as a J-cut. |
| 2.5-6 | **Setup:** stakes and constraint, shown | One or two shots. No backstory. |
| 6-26 | **Mechanism beats** (3-4 beats) | Each beat asks a mini-question and answers it. At every seam, a **rehook** closes that question and opens the next, about every 5-8 s: escalation, a new obstacle, a twist, a counter update, a specific promise. Show it as well as say it. See "Rehooks" in `hooks-and-rehooks.md`. |
| 26-32 | **Payoff** | The reveal or reaction the hook promised. |
| 32-33.5 | **Abrupt end** | One to three words, cut on the reaction. No outro. |

### Ending recipes

- **The one-word verdict:** "Wow." Cut. (Jenny's blind tests.)
- **Sentence loop:** the last line ends mid-thought, and the first line completes it on replay.
  "...and that's exactly why" then "I spent $1 on a burger".
- **Visual loop:** the last frame matches the first frame's composition, a match cut across the seam.
- **The reaction cut:** cut at the peak of the reaction, before it resolves.

Avoid "thanks for watching", subscribe asks, fades, music tails and recap lines.

## 5. Script rules

- Write for the ear: one idea per sentence, about 12 words or fewer.
- Every line either raises a question or answers one. Delete lines that only connect ("so", "now
  let's see") or announce what is coming.
- Use small, concrete numbers, at most about one per beat. "$1" beats "a 73% cost reduction".
- Use plain words: "making money", not "business finance".
- Read it aloud at the target speed. If it runs long, **cut lines; don't speed up**.
- Check it with `python scripts/readability.py script.txt --target 33.5`. Aim for FK grade 5 or
  below, no hard filler, and a hook line under about 2.5 s.

## 6. First frame and shot plan

**First-frame spec.** Describe it precisely enough for someone else to shoot or generate it:

- **Subject:** what it is, center-weighted, in the middle band of the screen (about 35-65% of the
  height).
- **Context cue:** a logo, sign, place or recognisable object, usually above the subject, but below
  the top UI strip.
- **Text:** the exact words (three or fewer, or one number), the size (readable at 180 px wide), the
  position (inside the safe zones), and high contrast.
- **Contrast and colour:** the subject separated from the background. Check the squint view.
- **Face or no face**, and why.
- A small sketch as a 3x3 grid in text is fine.

**Shot list.** For each shot, give the time range, what is in it, the framing, the on-screen text,
and the transition **out** of it, with its reason (cut on action, match cut, J-cut). Plan the loop
seam explicitly. Plan captions for the whole video.

## 7. Paper review

Before anything gets made, score the brief with `review-rubric.md` as if it were a finished video.
Use the first frame spec, the hook package, the beat sheet and the script. Any dimension under 7 in
first frame, hook or story goes back to step 3. Note the risks you cannot judge on paper, such as
the actual reaction or the lighting, as things to check in the first cut.

## Cutting a short from a long video

The most common failure: the short is the long video's first 30 seconds. A long-form opening is built
to set up a 10-minute payoff, so it promises ("let's look at what actually happened") and stops
before paying anything off. Build the short backwards instead:

1. **Find the payoff in the long video.** The single most surprising result, number, reaction or
   reveal. Note its timestamps.
2. **Write a new hook** that promises exactly that payoff, using the hook package. It often needs
   one new line or shot; everything else usually exists.
3. **Pick 2-4 mechanism beats** from the long video that raise the stakes on the payoff, each one a
   mini-question and its answer.
4. **Delete every signpost.** "Today", "first", "let's look at", "in this episode", chapter
   intros. They belong to the long format.
5. **Tighten the pauses.** Long-form delivery breathes; a short cannot. Cut gaps to about 0.1-0.15 s
   and hide the trims with punch-ins.
6. **End on the payoff**, abruptly. The long video's next section is not your ending.

In a review, the signs are signpost lines, a promise with no answer, and an ending on a definition or
setup line. Diagnose it, then point to where the payoff sits in the source.

## 8. Worked example

### English: "Is a cheap umbrella worth it?"

- Wrappers: (constraint) "$3 umbrella vs a $60 one in a storm"; (doubter) "My dad says expensive
  umbrellas are a scam, so we test his"; (test) "We drop both in a wind tunnel, blind."
- Winning hook package:
  - Line: "My dad says a $60 umbrella is a scam." (8 words, about 2 s)
  - Frame 1: Dad, centered, holding two closed umbrellas crossed like swords, with "$3" and "$60" tags
    big on each. Grey storm sky behind.
  - Text: "$3 vs $60"
  - Preview at 1.2 s: the cheap umbrella flipping inside out, for 0.6 s.
- Mechanism: three rounds (rain, wind, a gust from a leaf blower), with the score on screen.
- Rehooks: "Loser goes in the trash." (4.5 s, stakes); "Rain is easy." (10.5 s, escalation); "...and
  holds?" (17 s, twist); "Then Dad got the leaf blower." (18.5 s, a new obstacle shown and said, with
  the cut on "Then"). The full second-by-second map is in `hooks-and-rehooks.md`, section 11.
- Payoff: the final gust, with one umbrella standing. Dad's face. "...Okay." Cut.
- Loop: the last frame is Dad's face. Frame 1 is Dad's face with the umbrellas.
